\begin{frame}{The one-sentence version}
  \begin{block}{This week}
    I collected randomized orchestrator trajectories, cloned the best ones
    into a Qwen2.5-7B LoRA, and ran that policy against a frozen rule on the
    validation queries.
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Randomized rollouts.} 300 train trajectories. 167 actually had evidence.
    \item \textbf{Behavior cloning.} 113 state--action rows from the top 25\% by reward.
    \item \textbf{LoRA.} 3 epochs on an A100. Dev parses at 100\%. No invented evidence ids.
    \item \textbf{Val, $n=8$.} RACE is a tie ($+0.003$). FACT support is higher for the fixed rule ($0.72$ vs.\ $0.64$). Learned costs about twice the tokens and the time.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  Test queries 51--55 were not used to pick anything.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the orchestrator decides}
  After every research round the policy reads the evidence pool and writes
  exactly three lines.
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Action $(u, m, w)$}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{KEEP} --- which pages stay in context
        \item \texttt{ALLOC} --- how the next search budget is split
        \item \texttt{DECISION} --- \texttt{CONTINUE} or \texttt{TERMINATE}
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Same text online and offline}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The fork and the BC builder share one serializer.
        \item The prompt the model sees at eval is the prompt it was trained on.
        \item A bad answer does not crash the run: it keep-alls and continues, and the log says \texttt{parse\_failure}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  {\small Source: \texttt{gpt\_researcher/orchestration/serialize.py}. Eight features per page: relevance, redundancy, coverage, length, novelty, depth, round, new-or-old.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{$(u, m, w)$ in plain language}
  One research round ends. The policy must return three things. The letters are the paper's names for them.
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{tabular}{llp{7.2cm}}
    \hline
    Letter & In the log & Meaning \\
    \hline
    $u$ & \texttt{DECISION} & Stop and write the report, or search again. \\
    $m$ & \texttt{KEEP} & The set of page ids that stay in the working context. Everything else is dropped. \\
    $w$ & \texttt{ALLOC} & How the next round's searches are split across the open branches. Weights sum to 1. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  If $u$ is \texttt{TERMINATE}, $w$ is ignored: there is no next search. The report is written from the pages in $m$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The 8 numbers on each page}
  \small
  Order is fixed: \texttt{rho nov red cov tok depth age src}. From \texttt{features.py}.
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \begin{tabular}{llp{6.4cm}}
    \hline
    Name & Symbol & Definition \\
    \hline
    Relevance & $\rho$ & Cosine of this page with the root question. \\
    Novelty & nov & $1 -$ cosine with the most similar page already kept. \\
    Redundancy & red & Fraction of other pages with cosine $> 0.85$. \\
    Coverage & cov & Fraction of sub-questions this page matches (cosine $> 0.50$). \\
    Length & tok & Estimated tokens, $\lfloor \mathrm{chars}/4 \rfloor$. \\
    Depth & depth & Tree level that retrieved it. \\
    Age & age & Rounds since it was retrieved. \\
    Source & src & 2 = .gov/.edu, 1 = .org or Wikipedia, 0 = anything else. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  Two constants, not learned: $\tau = 0.85$ (near-duplicate) and $\tau_c = 0.50$ (covers a sub-question). Branch \texttt{gap} $= 1 - $ best cosine between a kept page and that branch's subquery.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What a trajectory is}
  A trajectory is the whole run of one question, not one decision.
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Planner writes sub-questions.
    \item For each round: search, scrape, then one $(u, m, w)$.
    \item If $u$ is continue, the next round searches only the branches $w$ funded, and the pool is whatever $m$ kept plus the new pages.
    \item If $u$ is terminate, the writer turns the kept pages into the report.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{0.35em}
  Query 88's learned trajectory has \textbf{one} round, then a report. A deeper one (learned 64) has \textbf{six} rounds: six keep-sets, six allocations, and a terminate on the last.
\end{frame}

  reports/88.md               the article that was scored

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The frozen rule, exactly}
  Val fixed-policy command pins four of the eleven knobs. The other seven are still drawn once per question from seed 0.
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \begin{verbatim}
GR_RANDOM_PARAMS = {
  "keep_ratio": 0.8,
  "min_rounds": 2,
  "p_terminate": 1.0,
  "temperature": 0.02
}
  \end{verbatim}
  \vspace{0.15em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{keep\_ratio 0.8.} On a pool of 47, keep $\lceil 0.8 \times 47 \rceil = 38$ pages. Not ``keep 80\% of the internet.''
    \item \textbf{temperature 0.02.} Almost no ranking noise. The order is the weighted score.
    \item \textbf{min\_rounds 2.} Cannot stop after round 1, even if coverage is already high.
    \item \textbf{p\_terminate 1.0.} As soon as round 2 is allowed, the coin flip is heads with probability 1. It always stops there.
  \end{itemize}
  So the frozen rule means: run two rounds, keep about 80\% each time, then write the report. Feature weights still depend on the question's seed. They are not a second learned model.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{1. Why the rollouts are randomized}
  The Table-4 baselines (\texttt{none}, \texttt{topk}, \ldots) are deterministic given the state.
  Every trajectory of \texttt{topk} keeps the same kind of page. There is no action diversity, so there is nothing to clone.
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \texttt{RandomizedPolicy} (\texttt{orchestration/randomized.py}) does the opposite:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Sample \textbf{11 orchestration parameters once} per query.
    \item Reuse that same vector on every later round of that query.
    \item The decision is still a heuristic (rank, allocate, stop). The randomness is in \emph{how aggressive} the heuristic is.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  Ten seeds on one question therefore span ``keep almost everything'' through ``keep 15\%'', and ``stop at round 1'' through ``keep going''.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How one rollout is launched}
  \texttt{adr rollouts} does not run 300 queries inside one process.
  gpt-researcher's token counters are process-global, so each rollout is its own Python process.
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Plan every pair $(\text{query}, \text{seed})$ from the train split. Seeds are $0 \ldots 9$.
    \item \texttt{run\_id} is \texttt{<qid>-s<seed>}, for example \texttt{57-s3}.
    \item Skip the pair if \texttt{trajectories/<qid>.json} already has a report and no error. The corpus is resumable.
    \item Subprocess: \texttt{adr run} with\\
      \texttt{GR\_ORCHESTRATOR=random} and \texttt{GR\_ORCH\_SEED=<seed>}.
    \item After the report, RACE is judged one rollout at a time. Two judges at once would overwrite the same file.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  {\small Driver: \texttt{src/adr/rollouts/driver.py}. Config: \texttt{configs/rollouts\_drb\_random\_d3b4.yaml}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The 11 parameters, sampled once}
  \small
  From \texttt{PARAM\_SPACE} in \texttt{randomized.py}. Log-uniform means the sampler draws uniformly in log space, so small and large values are both reachable.
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \hline
    Name & Draw & What it does \\
    \hline
    \texttt{keep\_ratio} & Unif$(0.15, 1)$ & Fraction of the pool to keep \\
    \texttt{budget\_frac} & Unif$(0.3, 1)$ & Token cap on the kept set \\
    \texttt{temperature} & LogUnif$(0.02, 0.5)$ & How much Gumbel noise shakes the ranking \\
    \texttt{w\_nov, w\_red, w\_cov} & Unif$(0, 1)$ & Feature weights \\
    \texttt{w\_len} & Unif$(0, 0.5)$ & Penalty on long pages \\
    \texttt{alloc\_concentration} & LogUnif$(0.5, 20)$ & Peaked vs.\ flat branch split \\
    \texttt{min\_rounds} & $\{1,2,3\}$ & Cannot stop before this round \\
    \texttt{p\_terminate} & Unif$(0, 0.25)$ & Coin-flip stop, after \texttt{min\_rounds} \\
    \texttt{phi\_gain\_stop} & Unif$(0, 0.05)$ & Stop if coverage gain is tiny \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the code does with those parameters}
  \textbf{Keep.} Score each page
  \[
    \rho + w_{\mathrm{nov}}\mathrm{nov} - w_{\mathrm{red}}\mathrm{red} + w_{\mathrm{cov}}\mathrm{cov} - w_{\mathrm{len}}\widehat{\mathrm{tok}}
  \]
  add Gumbel noise scaled by \texttt{temperature}, and keep the top $\lceil \texttt{keep\_ratio}\cdot n\rceil$ that still fit under the token cap.
  \vspace{0.35em}
  \textbf{Allocate.} Dirichlet whose mean follows each open branch's coverage gap. High \texttt{alloc\_concentration} puts almost all of the next search on the biggest gap.
  \vspace{0.35em}
  \textbf{Stop.} Only after \texttt{min\_rounds}. Then stop on a Bernoulli(\texttt{p\_terminate}) draw, or if the coverage gain fell below \texttt{phi\_gain\_stop}.
  \vspace{0.35em}
  The seed is \texttt{sha256(GR\_ORCH\_SEED | question)}, so seed 3 on query 57 is the same trajectory if you rerun it.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What gets written, and why BC can replay it}
  Each round's \texttt{decision.meta} stores the parameters, the eight features of every page, the frontier gaps, $\Phi$ before and after, and the seed.
  \vspace{0.3em}
  The offline builder reads that meta and calls the \textbf{same} \texttt{serialize\_state} the online policy uses. The training prompt is not a reconstruction from memory. It is the string the policy would have been shown.
  \vspace{0.4em}
  On disk, one rollout is a folder:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{57-s0/trajectories/57.json} --- harness steps and the report
    \item \texttt{57-s0/gpt\_researcher/57.json} --- the fork's rounds and \texttt{meta}
    \item \texttt{57-s0/metrics/summary.json} --- tokens, reads, retained pages
    \item \texttt{logs/57-s0.log} --- the subprocess log
    \item \texttt{manifest.jsonl} --- one row per rollout: done / failed
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{4. Fixed rule, the baseline}
  Same randomized code as the corpus. Four knobs pinned on every val question (see the earlier slide): keep 80\%, almost no ranking noise, at least 2 rounds, then always stop.
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item It is not the learned model, and it is not the average of the 300 train runs.
    \item The other seven knobs (feature weights, branch concentration, \ldots) are still sampled once per question from seed 0. Same question, same weights, if you rerun it.
    \item All \textbf{10/10} val queries finished with a report and real pages.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  On query 88 this rule kept 46 pages and pruned 30\%, across the two forced rounds. The learned policy kept 37 and stopped after one round.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked example: query 64}
  \textbf{Question.} UAV attitude control is usually one cascaded PID, and that setting only works in one flight regime. How should the parameters be chosen across flight states?
  \vspace{0.25em}
  Learned policy, six decisions. Every one parsed (\texttt{parse\_failure=false}). The last stop reason is \texttt{model}.
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{rrrrll}
    \hline
    round & pool & kept & dropped & decision & allocation \\
    \hline
    1 & 39 & 36 & 3 & CONTINUE & 0.11, 0.05, 0.41, 0.43 \\
    2 & 50 & 47 & 3 & CONTINUE & 0.11, 0.44, 0.45 \\
    3 & 75 & 46 & 29 & CONTINUE & 0.30, 0.33, 0.37 \\

\begin{frame}{What I will not claim in the meeting}
